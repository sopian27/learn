# Psychology — Domain Pack

## Domain Name

Psychology

## Scope

The scientific study of mind and behavior as an academic/empirical discipline: history & research methods, biological bases of behavior, states of consciousness, sensation & perception, learning, memory, cognition/language/intelligence, human development across the lifespan, motivation & emotion, personality theory, social psychology, psychopathology (descriptive/classification only), and major therapeutic modalities (conceptual overview of evidence-based approaches).

Does **NOT** provide clinical diagnosis, therapy, or treatment of the learner or any real person. This domain is psychoeducation for scientific literacy and understanding — not a substitute for a licensed psychologist/psychiatrist. Flag this explicitly wherever it matters (mirroring `domains/personal-finance/DOMAIN.md`'s "confirm with a licensed professional" pattern for tax/estate content), especially in the Psychopathology and Therapy modules.

Does **NOT** duplicate already-registered domains:
- `domains/character-development/DOMAIN.md` (applied habit-building: humility, work ethic, respect) — this domain supplies underlying scientific theory those domains can draw on, but its own Applied Practice stays in the register of scientific analysis (case studies, research critique, theory application), not personal habit change.
- `domains/self-love/DOMAIN.md` (applied self-compassion practice).
- `domains/critical-thinking/DOMAIN.md` (general applied-reasoning skill) — this domain touches cognitive-bias content too, but from the empirical judgment-and-decision-making research angle (Kahneman/Tversky) rather than critical-thinking's applied-reasoning-skill angle.

## Trusted Sources / Research Priority

1. **American Psychological Association (APA)** — official guidelines (`Guidelines for the Undergraduate Psychology Major`), the Ethics Code, DSM-5-TR classification (used descriptively, never for diagnosing the learner or real people), and Division 12's list of empirically supported treatments.
2. **OpenStax Psychology 2e** — free, peer-reviewed introductory textbook explicitly aligned to the APA undergraduate guidelines; used as the backbone for topic sequencing and depth-per-topic calibration.
3. **The Noba Project** — peer-reviewed, modular open psychology textbook (100+ modules by academic contributors); used for supplementary depth and alternate explanations per topic.
4. Books/textbooks with a durable, peer-reviewed academic track record (e.g. Myers' *Psychology*, Gray & Bjorklund's *Psychology*) for deeper conceptual grounding beyond the open textbooks above.
5. **Simply Psychology** and similar explainer sites — useful for accessible summaries and quick definitions, but never treated as authoritative alone; always cross-check against sources 1–4, per `standards/UNIVERSAL_STANDARDS.md`'s Vetting a Source bar.

**House rule specific to this domain:** whenever a "classic" study or finding is introduced (e.g. Milgram, Stanford Prison Experiment, Asch), explicitly flag its current replication/ethics status per the Open Science Collaboration's replication crisis findings — teach the finding AND how confidently the field still holds it. Never present a famous-but-unreplicated finding as settled fact.

## Applied Practice Definition

Applying a psychological framework or research-methods lens to a concrete, real case every lesson — not passive reading. Examples: analyzing a real or realistic behavior/case through a named theory (e.g. explaining a habit through operant conditioning), critiquing a published study's methodology (design, sampling, confounds, effect size, replication status), running a small self-observation log and interpreting it through a course concept, or drafting a mini research proposal (hypothesis, method, ethical considerations) without actually collecting data on real people. Never involves diagnosing the learner or any real named individual — case material is either the learner's own non-clinical self-observations, anonymized/hypothetical vignettes, or published case studies from the literature.

## Project Tiers

- **Mini Project** (per module): a single-concept application — e.g. a behavior log analyzed through one module's theory, or a short critique of one published study using that module's methodological lens.
- **Intermediate Project** (per major section): a multi-module integrative case analysis — e.g. a cognitive-processes case study combining sensation/perception, learning, and memory, or a person-level profile combining development, motivation, and personality theory.
- **Capstone / Portfolio Project** (end of course): a full research-literacy artifact — a rigorous critical analysis of one real published psychology paper (methodology, statistics, claims vs. evidence, replication status) PLUS an integrative biopsychosocial case formulation of a hypothetical/anonymized case drawing on the whole course, presentable and saved to `portofolio/`.

## Review Style

On top of the Universal Review Rubric (Strengths / Weaknesses / Actionable Improvements / Score 0–100), every review additionally checks:

- **Scientific Rigor** — is the reasoning grounded in evidence/theory from the Trusted Sources, or does it lean on folk psychology / pop-psych myths?
- **Theory Application Accuracy** — is the named framework/theory actually applied correctly, not just name-dropped?
- **Ethical Awareness** — where case material or study design is involved, does it reflect APA Ethics Code principles (e.g. confidentiality, beneficence, informed consent)?
- **Bias & Evidence-Quality Awareness** — does the work recognize common traps: small/WEIRD samples, correlation-vs-causation, unreplicated "classic" findings, p-hacking?

## Assessment Form

A short concept-check quiz after each lesson's theory portion, plus a "myth vs. fact" check before Applied Practice begins — testing whether a popular-psychology claim related to the lesson is actually supported by the evidence (per Lilienfeld et al.'s mythbusting tradition), before the learner applies the real concept.

## Practitioner Reference Frame

Compare how a research psychologist (peer-reviewed empirical approach), a licensed clinical psychologist (APA Division 12 empirically-supported-treatment approach, e.g. CBT/ACT), and the pop-psychology/self-help industry would each approach the same question or claim — highlighting differences in evidence quality and epistemic humility. Reference figures across subfields: Wundt/James (founding), Skinner (behaviorism), Piaget/Erikson/Bowlby (development/attachment), Bandura (social-cognitive), Kahneman & Tversky (judgment/decision-making), Beck (CBT), Seligman (positive psychology). Reference organizations: APA, British Psychological Society (BPS), NIMH, WHO (ICD-11 as the alternate classification to DSM-5-TR).
