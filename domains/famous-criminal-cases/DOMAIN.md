# Famous Criminal Cases — Domain Pack

## Domain Name

Famous Criminal Cases (Kasus Kriminal Terkenal Dunia)

## Scope

The broad true-crime case-study genre studied as forensic science, investigative methodology, and legal-system literacy — not as entertainment. Covers: why certain cases become "famous" and the selection/media bias baked into that (Module 1); timeline reconstruction and evidence chain-of-custody methodology (Module 2); forensic science fundamentals (fingerprints, DNA, ballistics) alongside its documented failure modes — bite-mark analysis, hair microscopy, arson science (Module 3); investigative psychology, motive typologies, and offender-profiling methodology with its documented accuracy limits (Module 4, kept intentionally shallow on individual-offender psychology — see boundary below); evidentiary standards and the legal trial process, from rules of evidence to jury dynamics (Module 5); landmark wrongful-conviction case studies documented by the Innocence Project (Module 6); media trial-by-coverage effects — how heavy coverage shapes or distorts public perception and sometimes the verdict itself (Module 7); organized/white-collar crime as a structurally different investigative problem (Module 8); cold cases resolved by modern forensic advances such as investigative genetic genealogy (Module 9); investigative errors and institutional accountability — tunnel vision, confirmation bias, prosecutorial misconduct (Module 10); the societal and legal impact of famous cases — laws and procedures that trace directly back to a single case (Module 11); and a capstone structured case-analysis framework applied to a case of the learner's choosing (Module 12).

Does **NOT** duplicate already-registered domains:
- `domains/serial-killers/DOMAIN.md` — the sibling domain, registered the same day. That domain deep-dives the academic forensic-psychology/neuroscience/developmental/behavioral profile of individual serial-murder offenders as case subjects (PCL-R, organized/disorganized typology applied case-by-case, neuroimaging findings, violentization theory, manipulation-tactic analysis). **This domain does not re-teach that depth.** This domain's Module 4 (Investigative Psychology, Motive & Profiling) covers profiling *methodology* and its *documented reliability limits* at the investigative-technique level (how profiling is used as an investigative tool, and why Snook et al. and similar academic critiques show it underperforms its popular reputation) — it does not build a psychological profile of any specific offender. When a case example used elsewhere in this domain (e.g. Module 9's genetic-genealogy module, where the Golden State Killer case is the canonical example) happens to involve a serial offender, it is used briefly to illustrate the forensic/legal/media point at hand, never for deep psychological profiling — that redirect belongs to `domains/serial-killers/DOMAIN.md`. This domain's primary case examples are deliberately drawn from non-serial-killer categories: wrongful convictions, organized/white-collar crime, notorious single-incident murder trials, and cold cases.
- `domains/psychology/DOMAIN.md` — supplies general psychopathology/personality theory. Neither this domain nor the serial-killers domain re-teaches that from scratch.
- `domains/media-literacy/DOMAIN.md` — supplies general source-evaluation method (SIFT, lateral reading, propaganda technique recognition). This domain's Module 7 (media trial-by-coverage) applies that general toolkit specifically to criminal-trial coverage rather than re-deriving it; lessons should cross-reference `domains/media-literacy/DOMAIN.md` rather than re-explaining SIFT/lateral reading from zero.
- `domains/critical-thinking/DOMAIN.md` — supplies general reasoning/evidence-quality/bias frameworks. The Module 12 capstone explicitly cross-references this domain's steelmanning, base-rate, and evidence-calibration principles rather than reinventing them.

Explicit framing constraint: this domain is forensic-science/legal/investigative-methodology literacy, never true-crime sensationalism, entertainment framing, or "whodunit" spectacle. Every case study centers documented evidence and institutional process, not narrative thrill. Victims are named with dignity, not reduced to plot devices.

## Trusted Sources / Research Priority

1. **National Academy of Sciences, *Strengthening Forensic Science in the United States: A Path Forward* (2009)** — the primary source for which forensic disciplines have a validated scientific foundation (DNA analysis) versus which have documented, serious validity problems (bite-mark analysis, hair microscopy comparison, arson-pattern "science," and to a lesser extent latent-fingerprint and ballistics comparison). Every forensic method taught in Module 3 must be taught alongside this report's findings on its actual error-rate evidence, not its courtroom reputation.
2. **The Innocence Project** (innocenceproject.org) — documented DNA exoneration case profiles and aggregate causal-factor data (eyewitness misidentification, false confession, informant testimony, forensic misapplication, government misconduct) for Module 6's wrongful-conviction case studies. Used for case facts and statistics only, cross-checked against court records where available.
3. **Brandon Garrett, *Convicting the Innocent: Where Criminal Prosecutions Go Wrong* (2011)** — the leading empirical academic study of the first 250 DNA exonerations, for rigorous causal analysis of wrongful convictions beyond anecdote.
4. **Court records, trial transcripts, and appellate opinions** — the primary legal source for any landmark-case claim (e.g. *Miranda v. Arizona*, a named wrongful-conviction case, a named white-collar prosecution). A claim about what happened in a trial must trace to the record, not to a documentary's dramatization.
5. **Investigative journalism with a documented editorial-rigor and correction record** (e.g. ProPublica's criminal-justice and forensic-science investigative series, long-form pieces from outlets with published corrections policies) — preferred over tabloid true-crime media, which is excluded as a source of factual claims entirely (it may be referenced only as an *object of study* in Module 7's media-effects lessons, never as a factual source).
6. **Radley Balko** (forensic-science accountability journalism, *The Cadaver King and the Country Dentist* co-authored with Tucker Carrington) — for the journalistic-accountability angle on forensic-science failures.
7. **DOJ Bureau of Justice Statistics / National Institute of Justice** — for base-rate criminal-justice system data (case outcomes, exoneration counts, forensic-backlog statistics) used to keep claims calibrated rather than anecdote-driven.
8. Cross-reference `domains/serial-killers/DOMAIN.md`'s source list only when a case example briefly touches a serial-offender case — never substitute it for this domain's own sources.

**House rule specific to this domain:** every case-study claim must be traceable to a court record, an Innocence Project case profile, a peer-reviewed/empirical study, or investigative journalism with a documented rigor standard — never to tabloid coverage, a true-crime documentary's dramatized reconstruction, or unverified online true-crime lore. This mirrors `domains/serial-killers/DOMAIN.md`'s primary-source-only rule, applied here to the broader case-study genre.

## Applied Practice Definition

Producing a structured, source-cited case-analysis artifact every lesson, applying that lesson's specific method to a real documented case — never a generic hypothetical. Examples: a timeline reconstruction annotated with chain-of-custody notes, a forensic-method validity audit (what does the NAS 2009 report actually say about this method's error rate), a structured write-up of one Innocence Project-documented exoneration identifying its causal factors, a media-coverage-effect analysis comparing pretrial coverage against trial outcome, a breakdown of one trial's evidentiary chain from admitted evidence to verdict, or a critique of one real profiling report's methodology against its documented accuracy limits.

## Project Tiers

- **Mini Project** (per module): a single-method application to one documented case — e.g. auditing one forensic technique's validity against the NAS 2009 report, or building one case's chain-of-custody timeline.
- **Intermediate Project** (per major section, roughly every 3-4 modules): a multi-module combined case study — e.g. a full wrongful-conviction report combining evidentiary review (Module 5) + forensic-method critique (Module 3) + investigative-error analysis (Module 10) for one Innocence Project-documented case.
- **Capstone / Portfolio Project** (end of course): a complete structured case-analysis report applying the course's full integrated framework (fame/bias check → timeline & chain of custody → forensic evidence audit → investigative methodology → evidentiary/legal process → media-effect analysis → societal impact → lessons learned) to one case of the learner's choosing, with an explicit sourcing appendix, presentable and saved to `portofolio/`.

## Review Style

On top of the Universal Review Rubric (Strengths / Weaknesses / Actionable Improvements / Score 0–100), every review additionally checks:

- **Source Rigor** — is every factual claim traceable to a court record, Innocence Project case profile, peer-reviewed study, or investigative journalism with a documented rigor standard — never tabloid/unverified true-crime lore?
- **Non-Sensationalism** — does the work stay in an analytical/institutional register, naming victims with dignity, with zero "whodunit"/entertainment framing?
- **Evidentiary Reasoning** — does the work correctly distinguish evidence strength (e.g. validated forensic science vs. contested methodology), the applicable burden of proof, and correlation from proof?
- **Bias Awareness** — does the work identify the media-selection, investigative-tunnel-vision, or cognitive biases actually operating in that case, rather than treating the outcome as inevitable in hindsight?

## Assessment Form

A short concept-check quiz after each lesson's theory portion, plus a "myth vs. fact" check before Applied Practice begins each module — testing a popular true-crime claim related to the lesson (e.g. "fingerprint matching is infallible," "a confession means the case is solved," "DNA evidence can't be wrong") against the documented evidence before the learner applies the real method. Mirrors `domains/serial-killers/DOMAIN.md`'s assessment structure.

## Practitioner Reference Frame

Compare how a forensic scientist bound by validated, Daubert-standard methodology would approach a case, against how an investigative journalist with a documented editorial-rigor standard (ProPublica-style) would approach the same case, against how a defense attorney/Innocence Project litigator would approach it, against how tabloid true-crime media would frame it — making the evidence-quality gap explicit every time. Reference figures: Barry Scheck & Peter Neufeld (Innocence Project co-founders), Brandon Garrett (empirical wrongful-conviction scholar), Radley Balko (forensic-accountability journalist), the National Academy of Sciences 2009 report's expert panel. Reference organizations: the Innocence Project, ProPublica, the National Institute of Justice.

## Domain-Specific Standards

- **Primary/documented-source-only rule**: every factual case claim must cite a traceable source (court record, Innocence Project case profile, peer-reviewed study, or rigor-documented investigative journalism) — never tabloid or unverified true-crime content. Tabloid/sensationalist coverage may be studied only as an *object of analysis* in Module 7, never cited as fact.
- **Non-sensationalism rule**: all case material stays in analytical/institutional framing. No "ranking" or spectacle framing of crimes; every case-study lesson names victims with dignity, not only the offender or the crime's shock value.
- **Serial-killer-depth boundary rule**: if a case example involves a serial offender, use it only briefly to illustrate a forensic/legal/media point — never for deep psychological profiling of that offender. Redirect any request for that depth to `domains/serial-killers/DOMAIN.md`.
- **Forensic-validity rule**: every forensic method introduced must be taught alongside its NAS 2009-documented validity status in the same lesson — never presented as infallible because it "sounds scientific" or appears authoritative on television.
- Otherwise, `standards/UNIVERSAL_STANDARDS.md` is sufficient — add `domains/famous-criminal-cases/standards/` only if a recurring need for domain-specific rigor rules emerges.
