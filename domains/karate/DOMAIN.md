# Karate — Domain Pack

## Domain Name

Karate

## Scope

Traditional karate as a striking martial art: history/philosophy, kihon (fundamentals: stances, strikes, kicks, blocks, footwork), kata (forms) and bunkai (application), kumite (sparring, from prearranged to free), physical conditioning specific to karate (flexibility, breathing, balance, timing), self-defense principles grounded in karate technique, dojo etiquette/discipline, and belt progression (kyu/dan) modeled on JKA Shotokan's syllabus as the primary reference frame, with Goju-ryu and Kyokushin covered comparatively (Module on style differences), not as parallel full syllabi. Does NOT cover: other striking arts (Muay Thai, Boxing, Taekwondo) except for brief comparative mention where useful; grappling arts (BJJ, Judo, Wrestling); weapons-based kobudo (bo, sai, nunchaku) beyond a brief historical mention; MMA competition strategy; or actual dojo certification/rank issuance (this course cannot award a real belt — it prepares the learner to test at a real dojo).

## Trusted Sources / Research Priority

1. JKA (Japan Karate Association) official grading syllabus and technical documents — primary reference for Shotokan kihon/kata/kumite progression and kyu-to-dan structure (most standardized, widely mirrored by affiliated dojos worldwide).
2. WKF (World Karate Federation) official Kata and Kumite Competition Rules (wkf.net/documents) — authoritative for the official kata list, competition kumite rules, and scoring, used whenever the course touches sport/competitive karate.
3. IKGA (International Okinawan Goju-ryu Karate-do Federation) and IKO (International Karate Organization, Kyokushin) official materials — used specifically for the style-comparison module, never mixed into the Shotokan-based core syllabus without attribution.
4. Gichin Funakoshi's own writings ("Karate-Do Kyohan", "Karate-Do: My Way of Life") — primary source for karate philosophy, the Dojo Kun, and the Niju Kun (20 precepts), since he is the direct source the modern Shotokan tradition traces to.
5. Reputable applied strength/conditioning and sports-science sources (e.g. published research on martial arts flexibility/conditioning, not fitness-influencer content) for the physical training module — cross-checked against source 1's own conditioning guidance rather than taken alone.
6. Established local/national dojo federations only when they cite back to sources 1-3 above; never treat a single commercial dojo's marketing page as authoritative on its own.

Never build a technical (kihon/kata/kumite) lesson from a single YouTube video or blog post alone — always triangulate against the JKA syllabus (source 1) or WKF rules (source 2).

## Applied Practice Definition

Physical practice performed and self-recorded by the learner every lesson — not just reading about technique. Concretely: performing the stance/strike/kick/block/kata described, ideally in front of a mirror or recorded on video for self-review, logging reps/sets, noting where form broke down, and (where the lesson is paired) practicing with a partner for kumite drills. A written-only reflection never substitutes for the physical rep; the course flags any lesson where physical practice cannot be verified (e.g. self-reported "I did it" with no specific observation) as incomplete.

**Safety boundary carried from the user's own framing**: teach technique safely, progress gradually, reference credible dojo/federation material, and never teach genuinely dangerous techniques (e.g. full-contact strikes to vulnerable targets, joint-breaking applications) without real supervision — those lessons explicitly say "requires a qualified instructor in person" instead of a how-to.

## Project Tiers

- **Mini Project** (per module): a specific, checkable physical outcome — e.g. 20 clean reps of oi-zuki (lunge punch) recorded and self-critiqued against JKA form criteria, or performing Heian Shodan kata from memory without stopping.
- **Intermediate Project** (per few modules): a combined kihon-into-kata-into-bunkai demonstration for one kata, or a full kyu-grading mock test (kihon + kata + kumite) self-assessed against the JKA syllabus for that belt level.
- **Capstone / Portfolio Project** (end of course): a recorded mock grading test at the target level the learner is aiming for (e.g. mock brown-belt or mock shodan test), covering kihon, kata, kumite/bunkai, and a written reflection connecting technique to the philosophy/discipline modules — saved to `portofolio/`, explicitly framed as preparation for a real dojo test, not a substitute for one.

## Review Style

On top of the Universal Review Rubric (Strengths / Weaknesses / Actionable Improvements / Score 0-100), every physical-practice review additionally checks:

- **Form Fidelity** — does the stance/strike/kick/kata match the referenced source (JKA syllabus/WKF kata) in the specific criteria that source calls out, not just "looks roughly right"?
- **Power Generation** — is power coming from hip rotation/weight transfer as the sources teach, not just arm/leg muscle?
- **Balance & Control** — is the learner stable through the technique (no wobbling, no over-committing past recoverable balance)?
- **Safety** — is the technique being practiced in a way that won't injure the learner (e.g. correct knee tracking in kicks, not hyperextending elbows on punches)?

## Assessment Form

Short concept-check questions after each lesson's theory portion (terminology, the "why" behind a technique or principle), plus a self-assessment against the four Review Style criteria above before submitting a Mini/Intermediate/Capstone Project. For belt-progression modules specifically, the assessment additionally mirrors the actual JKA kyu-test format (kihon set + kata + kumite) as a mock test.

## Practitioner Reference Frame

Compare how a JKA-lineage Shotokan instructor would teach a technique (linear power, deep stances, "one strike, one kill" — ikken hisatsu) against how a Goju-ryu instructor would teach the same situation (circular redirection, closer range, breathing-linked power — Sanchin) against how a Kyokushin instructor would (full-contact conditioning, minimal blocking, absorb-and-counter). Use Gichin Funakoshi (Shotokan founder) and his Niju Kun as the philosophical reference point throughout, since discipline/etiquette/self-control are as central to this domain as technique.

## Domain Goal

Learner masters karate fundamentals (kihon), can perform and explain the kata expected up through an advanced kyu grade (targeting brown belt / pre-shodan understanding), has a stronger and more disciplined body from consistent conditioning practice, and understands when and how to responsibly use self-defense ability — with clear judgment about proportionality and escalation, not just physical capability.
